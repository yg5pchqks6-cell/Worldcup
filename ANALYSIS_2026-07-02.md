# Diamond Edge Post-Mortem — July 2, 2026 Slate

## TL;DR

The model didn't lose because of bad luck. **At bet365's prices, every single moneyline
and total on this slate was -5% to -8.5% EV versus Pinnacle's no-vig fair line.** The
correct output for the day was "no bets." Instead the app reported an average *raw edge of
+17.6%* across 18 picks and crowned MIL ML @ 1.46 a 99/100-confidence "Best Play" — even
though its own robustness veto had already marked every card PASS / stake 0u. The model's
probabilities are systematically inflated, and the UI layer that picks the "Best Play"
ignores the veto layer that knew better.

average edge is measuring its own miscalibration.

## Loss #1 — CIN @ MIL: "MIL ML @ 1.46, conf 99/100" → CIN won 7-2

What the model said:
- Internal sim: MIL 69%. "Calibration" layer: 77% → **80%** (via "MIL hit rate 64%").
- Edge claimed: +16.3pp raw, +5.4pp boosted. Best Play banner, conf 99/100.

What the market said:
- Pinnacle no-vig: MIL **63.9%**. bet365's 1.46 implies 68.5% — the book was *shading*
  MIL, so the price was ~5pp worse than fair (-6.7% EV).
- The app's own card knew this: *"Book is shading this side ≥4pp beyond sharp fair —
  that's the book telling you something. Price vs sharp fair: -14.8pp."* Robustness veto
  fired → PASS, stake 0u. **The Best Play banner ignored the veto and promoted it anyway.**

Why the model was wrong on the baseball:
- The sim leaned on Misiorowski's 1.47 ERA. The app's own Savant row showed
  **MIL SP xERA 3.41 vs CIN SP xERA 3.48 — a dead-even true-talent matchup** (Burns:
  2.26 ERA, 13.3 K/9). The ΔxERA +1.94 regression flag for Misiorowski was displayed but
  never fed into the sim. He allowed 5 runs (2 HR) in 5 IP; Burns went 6 strong. A 1.46
  favorite priced internally at 80% (fair odds 1.25) is not a calibration nudge, it's a
  hallucination — no MLB team is an 80% favorite against a healthy ace.
- The "calibrated: 77% → 80% (MIL hit rate 64%)" step boosts probability using
  small-sample team hit rates. That is anti-calibration: it doubles down on exactly the
  bias being measured.

## Loss #2 — MIA @ COL: "MIA ML @ 1.72, 68%" → Rockies blowout (14-4)

What the model said:
- Sim MIA 7.54–5.84 (68% / 32%), raw edge +18.6pp, boosted +6.0pp.
- Card was PASS (veto: `non_positive_edge_vs_pinnacle; anchor_stale`), conf 63 — but the
  game-tile grid still displayed it as a GOOD-tier MIA lean with the +6.0pp boost.

What the market said: MIA fair was **55.2%**, and bet365's 1.72 was -5% EV. The market's
32% → 45% gap on COL was the Coors adjustment the model doesn't have.

Why the model was wrong on the baseball:
1. **Coors Field isn't in the model.** A 68% road favorite at Coors with a 13.4-run sim
   total is structurally miscalibrated: Coors inflates run variance enormously, and high
   variance compresses win probabilities toward 50%. The market never had COL below ~45%.
2. **The bullpen input was fiction.** The news layer itself surfaced: *"Miami's bullpen
   is significantly depleted with four relievers on the IL (Bender, Junk, Ekness, Nardi)."*
   Yet the sim used MIA effPen 3.50 / bullpen ERA 3.70 (#12) — the pre-injury numbers —
   and applied only a cosmetic headline multiplier (conf ×0.85, -0.25 runs). Lorenzen
   (6.97 ERA) vs Gusto (5.06 ERA, 5 GS) at Coors with a gutted pen behind Gusto is a
   recipe for exactly what happened: a crooked-innings blowout. The model's own
   "crooked-inning risk" flag existed — it just didn't subtract from anything.
3. **Placeholder data everywhere.** Across the slate the bullpen ERAs repeat suspiciously
   (away 4.20 / home 3.10 in six of nine games) — fallback constants, not live data. Every
   game card showed "DATA QUALITY: INCOMPLETE — 2-4 CRITICAL missing," and the effects
   engine ran with umpire/weather/starter-variance/defensive-K all `disabled`.

## Root causes, ranked

1. **Best Play banner bypasses the veto layer** (UI/ranking bug — this alone caused both
   losing tickets: every card said PASS/0u, the header said "bet MIL, conf 99").
2. **Edge is computed against the model's own inflated probability**, then "boosted" by a
   hit-rate calibration layer that pushes favorites further from market. Raw edges of
   +16 to +36pp on a normal slate are impossible; treat any model-vs-sharp gap > 4pp as
   evidence *against* the model.
3. **Sim uses ERA, not xERA/xwOBA** — its own Savant panel contradicted its two biggest
   convictions (Misiorowski 1.47 ERA vs 3.41 xERA; Bryce Miller 1.97 vs 7.12).
4. **No park model** — Coors must widen run distributions and compress ML probs.
5. **News/injury layer is cosmetic** — 4 IL relievers never touched the bullpen input.
6. **Silent fallback data** — repeated 4.20/3.10 bullpen constants, "2-4 CRITICAL
   missing" on every card, effects engine mostly disabled, yet confidence still hit 99.
